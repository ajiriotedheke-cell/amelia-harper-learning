FOR THE GROWN-UPS, TOO

Small moments. Big learning.

Welcome to the Amelia Harper Parent Learning Centre. Find practical ways to turn everyday play, reading, music and creative time into positive learning moments. These ideas are designed for parents and caregivers to adapt to each child's interests, pace and stage.

Explore printable resources →

Children learn in different ways. Keep activities relaxed, celebrate effort, and let children take breaks when they need them. An adult should supervise online play, listening and any activity involving small objects or craft materials.

AGES 2–4 · EARLY DISCOVERY

Learning through everyday play

Young children build early language, coordination and curiosity through short, repeated experiences with familiar people and objects.

Try together

  • Name colours and shapes during tidying or play.
  • Count a few toys as you put them away.
  • Share a picture book and invite your child to point out familiar objects.
  • Sing a simple action song and copy movements together.
AGES 5–7 · CURIOUS CREATORS

Build early reading and number confidence

Short, encouraging practice can help children connect letters, sounds, quantities and everyday problem-solving.

Try together

  • Look for a letter on food packaging or signs during a walk.
  • Make groups of objects and count them aloud.
  • Ask your child to retell the beginning, middle and end of a favourite story.
  • Use drawing and colouring to practise pencil control.
AGES 8–10 · BRIGHT ADVENTURERS

Encourage questions and independent thinking

Children can begin to explain how they reached an answer, compare ideas and plan small creative projects.

Try together

  • Ask what clues helped solve a puzzle or game challenge.
  • Keep a simple nature or reading journal.
  • Plan a small craft project and list the materials needed.
  • Talk about a story character's choices and feelings.
AGES 11–12 · SUPER THINKERS

Support creativity and thoughtful choices

Older children benefit from opportunities to research, create, reflect and discuss how they use digital media.

Try together

  • Invite your child to design a quiz or teach you a new fact.
  • Compare two sources and discuss how to check information.
  • Set shared screen-time expectations and take regular breaks.
  • Encourage a personal project such as a short story, poster or simple model.
Make screen time part of a balanced day.
Choose age-appropriate activities, stay nearby, talk about what your child is watching or playing, and balance digital experiences with sleep, movement, outdoor play and face-to-face time.

STORIES & MUSIC

Read, listen and talk together

After a story, ask what your child enjoyed, which character they remember, or what they might do in a similar situation. Songs can also open conversations about kindness, gratitude and daily routines.
