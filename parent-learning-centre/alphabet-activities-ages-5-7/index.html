10 Playful Alphabet Activities for Children Aged 5–7

Try simple letter-recognition, sound and early-writing activities at home with everyday objects and short games.

Amelia Harper Kids · 5-minute read

Children aged five to seven are often ready to explore how letters look, how they sound and how they combine to make words. The most useful practice does not need to feel like a lesson. Short, playful activities built into reading, cooking, drawing and everyday routines can make letters feel familiar.

Ten simple alphabet activities

  1. Letter hunt: Choose one letter and look for it on food packets, book covers or signs. Point out both uppercase and lowercase forms.
  2. Name detective: Find the first letter in each family member's name. Invite your child to notice letters that appear more than once.
  3. Sound basket: Gather a few safe household objects that begin with the same sound. Say the word slowly and listen for its first sound.
  4. Alphabet path: Write a few large letters on paper and place them on the floor. Call out a letter and let your child step beside it.
  5. Playdough letters: Roll playdough into lines and curves to form a letter. An adult should supervise younger children and small materials.
  6. Picture and letter match: Match familiar picture cards to their starting letters. Focus on the sound rather than speed.
  7. Story spotting: During shared reading, choose a letter to spot on a page. Keep the story enjoyable; you do not need to stop at every occurrence.
  8. Letter collage: Draw a large letter and decorate it with drawings or paper shapes of things that start with its sound.
  9. Alphabet rhythm: Clap or tap gently while saying a short sequence of letters, then pause and invite your child to continue.
  10. Make a mini word book: Fold paper into a small booklet. Add one familiar letter and a drawing or word to each page.

Keep the practice encouraging

Children do not all learn letters at the same pace. Choose a small number of letters at a time, revisit them in different contexts and celebrate effort. If a child mixes up letters, calmly model the correct shape or sound and move on. Avoid turning practice into a timed test.

Connect activities with digital play

For a short screen-based activity, try ABC Letter Adventure. It offers an interactive alphabet challenge with selectable age bands. Sit with your child, discuss the prompts and take breaks when needed. You can also browse the learning resources for the featured alphabet and numbers workbook.

Make it part of everyday life

Letters appear on labels, calendars, books, menus and signs. Invite your child to notice them during ordinary routines. A few relaxed minutes of shared attention can be more enjoyable than a long worksheet session. Pair letter activities with drawing, conversation, reading aloud and plenty of offline play.

A gentle reminder: Keep activities playful and pressure-free. Adapt them to your child's interests, abilities and attention, and supervise online and hands-on activities.
