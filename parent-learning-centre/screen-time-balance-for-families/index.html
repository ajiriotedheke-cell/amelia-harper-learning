A Practical Guide to Balanced Screen Time for Families

Plan thoughtful screen use with clear routines, adult involvement, offline alternatives and age-appropriate activities.

Amelia Harper Kids · 5-minute read

Digital games and videos can be one part of a child's day, but they should sit alongside sleep, movement, conversation, reading, school, family time and outdoor play. A useful family screen-time plan is clear, flexible and based on the child's age, needs and the type of content being used.

Build a family media routine

  1. Agree on screen-free moments. Families may choose meals, bedtime preparation or shared conversations as times to put devices away.
  2. Choose content intentionally. Look for activities that match the child's age, interests and current needs rather than autoplaying whatever appears next.
  3. Stay involved. Sit nearby, ask questions and talk about what the child is playing or watching. Shared use gives adults a chance to understand the experience.
  4. Plan an ending. Give a clear warning before a session ends and decide what happens next, such as a snack, outdoor play or a book.
  5. Make room for movement. Alternate seated activities with stretching, dancing, walking or active play.
  6. Review the routine together. As children grow, revisit the plan and explain the reasons behind family boundaries.

Choose interactive activities thoughtfully

When selecting an educational game, look at the actual activity rather than relying only on its label. Does it invite problem-solving, letter recognition, memory or creativity? Can the child stop easily? Are instructions understandable? Does it avoid pressuring children to buy items or share personal information?

Explore and discuss games together

Amelia Harper Kids offers several browser activities, including ABC Letter Adventure, Magical Memory Match, Dinosaur Discovery and the 3D Ocean of Wonders. Adults can help children choose an appropriate challenge, talk about what they notice and take breaks.

Offline alternatives make the plan easier

Keep a short list of easy options: drawing, building with blocks, reading, simple puzzles, helping prepare food with adult supervision, nature walks or imaginative play. A screen-time plan is easier to follow when children know what they can do next.

Make adjustments when needed

There is no single schedule that fits every family. Consider the child's mood, daily routine, accessibility needs and family circumstances. If media use is regularly disrupting sleep, school, relationships or wellbeing, discuss the concern with a qualified professional.

A gentle reminder: Keep activities playful and pressure-free. Adapt them to your child's interests, abilities and attention, and supervise online and hands-on activities.
