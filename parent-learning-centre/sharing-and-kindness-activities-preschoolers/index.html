How to Teach Sharing and Kindness to Preschoolers

Use everyday play, modelling, turn-taking and stories to help preschool children practise kindness and cooperation.

Amelia Harper Kids · 5-minute read

Sharing and kindness are skills children practise over time. Preschoolers may understand the idea of taking turns but still find it difficult when they are excited, tired or attached to a favourite toy. Adults can help by modelling the behaviour, naming feelings and creating simple opportunities to practise.

Practical ways to practise kindness

  • Model the words: Say “Would you like a turn when I am finished?” and “Thank you for waiting.” Children learn from hearing respectful language used naturally.
  • Use a turn-taking signal: A small timer or a clear “my turn, your turn” routine can help make waiting more understandable. Keep turns short for young children.
  • Notice helpful actions: Describe what happened: “You helped put the blocks away.” Specific feedback is clearer than a general demand to be good.
  • Practise with pretend play: Use dolls, toy animals or figures to act out taking turns, welcoming someone or helping a friend.
  • Talk about feelings: Ask how a character might feel when left out or when someone offers help. Accept the child's answer and add your own perspective gently.
  • Create small family jobs: Invite children to pass napkins, water a plant with help or put toys in a basket.

What to do when a child does not want to share

Avoid forcing a child to hand over a treasured object immediately. Set a clear boundary around safety and fairness, acknowledge the feeling, and help children agree on a turn or choose another activity. Adults may need to stay close and guide the exchange. Sharing is not the same as giving up every personal belonging on demand.

Use stories, songs and play

After a story, talk about a character's choices and what might help a friendship. The Good Manners Songs page describes the themes planned for the collection, including kindness, friendship, gratitude and respect; it currently lists themes rather than playable tracks. Children can also practise gentle care in Pet Planet, where the play activities centre on looking after a virtual pet.

Celebrate progress without pressure

Children need repeated chances to practise. Notice small steps such as waiting briefly, offering help or using polite words. Keep expectations age-appropriate and remember that adults are still responsible for setting safe boundaries and helping children resolve disagreements.

A gentle reminder: Keep activities playful and pressure-free. Adapt them to your child's interests, abilities and attention, and supervise online and hands-on activities.
