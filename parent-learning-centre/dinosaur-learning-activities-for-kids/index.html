Dinosaur Learning Activities for Curious Children

Combine dinosaur-themed reading, sorting, drawing, fossil play and simple research to encourage curiosity beyond the screen.

Amelia Harper Kids · 5-minute read

Dinosaur themes can turn an ordinary afternoon into a chance to ask questions, sort information, draw, build and imagine. Children do not need specialist equipment. Paper, crayons, toy figures, books and safe household materials can support a simple dinosaur activity session.

Six dinosaur activities to try

  1. Make a dinosaur fact card. Choose a dinosaur from a book and draw it. With an adult's help, add its name and one fact from the source.
  2. Sort by features. Group toy dinosaurs or pictures by features such as long necks, horns, plates or body size. Invite the child to explain the grouping.
  3. Create a paper fossil. Press a toy footprint into modelling material suitable for the child's age, following the material's safety instructions and supervising throughout.
  4. Build a prehistoric habitat. Use blocks, paper plants and toy figures to create an imagined landscape. Ask what the dinosaur might need in its environment.
  5. Tell a discovery story. Invite the child to imagine finding a fossil. What clues might they notice? What questions would they ask?
  6. Draw a skeleton puzzle. Draw simple shapes for a dinosaur body and rearrange paper pieces to explore how parts fit together.

Encourage careful questions

Children may ask how scientists know what dinosaurs looked like or what they ate. Look together at a children's book or a trusted museum or science resource. Explain the difference between a fossil clue, a scientific interpretation and an imaginative story. It is fine to say that you do not know and look for an answer together.

Try Dinosaur Discovery

The Dinosaur Discovery game invites players to uncover fossil pieces and complete a dinosaur-themed challenge. Play alongside your child and ask them to describe the clues they notice. Follow the game with an offline drawing or fact-card activity to extend the experience.

Keep the activity flexible

Younger children may enjoy naming and sorting pictures, while older children may want to compare information or make a small presentation. Let the child's curiosity guide the next step. Keep materials age-appropriate and supervise any activity involving small pieces or craft tools.

A gentle reminder: Keep activities playful and pressure-free. Adapt them to your child's interests, abilities and attention, and supervise online and hands-on activities.
