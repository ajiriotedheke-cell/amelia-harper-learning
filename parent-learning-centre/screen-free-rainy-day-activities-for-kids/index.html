15 Screen-Free Indoor Activities for Rainy Days Using Everyday Household Items

Keep children curious and busy indoors with low-cost crafts, movement games, pretend play and simple learning activities.

Ages 2–10 · 5–7 minute read · Family activities and practical guidance

Rainy days can make the whole family feel stuck indoors. You do not need an expensive craft kit or a perfectly organised playroom to make the day enjoyable. A few everyday objects, a little space and activities that can be adapted by age are often enough. Choose activities that match your child's interests and supervise anything involving small objects, scissors, water or movement.

Creative play

1. Make a cardboard-box house or vehicle. 2. Create a collage from scrap paper and child-safe glue. 3. Draw a family comic strip. 4. Make paper puppets and invent a short show. 5. Build a tower from safe, clean containers. Invite children to describe what they are making and why.

Movement and sensory play

6. Set up a cushion path on the floor, leaving clear space and avoiding unstable furniture. 7. Play freeze dance. 8. Try an indoor animal-movement game. 9. Sort large household objects by colour or texture. 10. Make a supervised water-transfer activity using a small amount of water, cups and a towel. Keep water away from electrical items.

Thinking and language games

11. Play “I spy” with colours or shapes. 12. Hide a familiar object and give clues. 13. Tell a story one sentence at a time. 14. Match pairs of socks or picture cards. 15. Make a pretend shop and practise counting, turn-taking and polite requests. Use large objects with toddlers to avoid choking hazards.

Make the activity easier to start

Put only a few materials out at once. Demonstrate the first step, then let your child take over. A ten-minute activity is still worthwhile; children do not need to remain engaged with one idea all afternoon. Rotate between active play, quiet play, snacks, reading and rest.

Let children lead the next round

When an activity ends, ask what they would change or invent. A cardboard box can become a bus, a shop or a rocket. Their ideas are part of the learning. Avoid turning every game into a test or worksheet.

Adapt it by age
Ages 2–4: choose short activities with large safe materials, simple sorting, songs and pretend play; supervise closely. Ages 5–10: add planning challenges, storytelling, simple measuring, construction and cooperative games. Adapt the difficulty rather than expecting every child to enjoy the same activity.

Try it together

For a printable follow-up, browse Learning Resources. The ABC Letter Adventure can be an optional short digital activity after screen-free play, rather than the centre of the rainy-day plan.

A gentle reminder
The goal is connection and curiosity, not keeping children occupied every minute. Choose safe materials, follow your child's lead and allow unstructured time between activities.
